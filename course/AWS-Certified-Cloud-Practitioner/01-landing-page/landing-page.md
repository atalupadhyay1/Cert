## ❓ Frequently asked questions

**Is this the real exam?**
No. These are original practice questions written for this course. Nothing is copied from or recalled from a live exam, and no question bank was used as a source.

**How many questions are on the real exam?**
AWS publishes **65 questions in 90 minutes**, of which **50 are scored and 15 are unscored**; AWS does not identify which items are unscored. Each set here is 65 items, matching that length and pace.

**What is the passing score?**
AWS publishes a **minimum passing score of 700 on a scaled range of 100–1000**. Scoring is compensatory, with no separate pass mark per domain. Because 700 is scaled rather than a raw percentage, this course does not convert practice results into an expected exam score.

**Why are there no case studies?**
Because the published CLF-C02 format has none. The exam is multiple choice and multiple response only, so no case-study scaffolding is included or imitated.

**How many multiple-response items are on the real exam?**
AWS does not publish a split between multiple choice and multiple response. Each set here carries **55 multiple-choice and 10 multiple-response** items; that mix is an authoring choice and is labelled as one everywhere it appears.

**Does the exam test configuration or coding?**
No. AWS lists coding, architecture design, troubleshooting, implementation, and load and performance testing as out of scope for this exam. Items ask you to recognise a concept or select a service for a stated requirement.

**Do I need hands-on experience?**
No prerequisites are published. AWS describes the typical candidate as having up to six months of AWS exposure in a line-of-business role, and states that no IT background is required.

**How should I use the six exams?**
Take Exam 1 untimed for a baseline, study the domains where you lost most items, then work through Exams 2–5 with a light clock and finish with Exam 6 under the full 90 minutes.

**Does a good score guarantee I will pass?**
No. Practice performance is not a scaled score, and no practice result predicts the outcome of a live appointment.

**Practice percentages are not scaled scores.** A score of 80% here means you answered 52 of 65 practice items correctly; it is not an estimate of your exam result.
