# Curriculum outline — AB-731 practice course

**Six 100-item mock exams · 600 original questions · 45-minute pace drill or 90-minute review**

This outline turns the bank into a study sequence. Each exam is blueprint-weighted **37 / 38 / 25**, so the order below is about scenario complexity and topic reinforcement, not about weighting.

Microsoft runs AB-731 as a **45-minute proctored assessment** and **does not publish an item count**. A 100-item exam is therefore an expanded practice bank, not a length replica: `6 × 100 = 600` lets each blueprint be drilled twice without repeating a question, and the real clock cannot be mapped item-for-item onto a practice attempt.

## Learning arc

| Phase | Exam | What it stresses | Study goal |
|---|---|---|---|
| Foundation | 1 | Value cases, generative versus classical AI, prompts, grounding, data quality, secure AI | Establish the vocabulary a leader is expected to use: token, grounding, drift, prompt, hallucination |
| Microsoft apps | 2 | Copilot mapping, editions, chat surfaces, Studio agents, Graph grounding | Separate what each Microsoft product can and cannot do without extra build |
| Judgment | 3 | Regulated operations, model risk documentation, licence tiers, commitment planning | Decide between similar-sounding options using the constraint in the stem |
| Operating context | 4 | Manufacturing and field adoption, safety review, superseded procedures, shift peaks | Place a human control where the consequence is physical or irreversible |
| Public accountability | 5 | Residency, eligibility decisions, accessibility, departmental adoption, procurement | Keep fairness, privacy, and authorization as three separate concerns |
| Integration | 6 | Everything, at group scale across brands | Perform under a 45-minute clock with mixed item formats |

## Exam-by-exam study plan

### Exam 1 — Cross-industry baseline (100 items)
- Official split: 37 / 38 / 25.
- Case studies: Fabrikam Retail, Northwind Insurance, Contoso Health, Adatum Bank.
- Focus: value case, classification versus generation, token economics, fabrication controls, prompt technique, retrieval, data quality, secure AI, ML lifecycle.
- Microsoft apps: mapping processes to Copilot, edition differences, chat experiences, in-app use, Studio agents, Graph grounding, build/buy/extend, model choice.
- Adoption: responsible AI principles, council, standards, champions, barriers, data and privacy impacts, licence types, commitment models.
- Target skill: state the deciding constraint before reading the options.

### Exam 2 — Health and life sciences (100 items)
- Case studies: Cedar Ridge Health, Brookfield Clinics, BioNova Labs, CareFirst Payer.
- Focus: clinical documentation volume, consent and privacy, retrieval over protocols that change, human review before clinical use, patient-facing chat boundaries.
- Microsoft apps: Copilot in clinical administration, Studio agents for intake, Graph grounding with permission trimming, Researcher for evidence reviews.
- Adoption: clinical champions, training on real tasks, escalation for harmful answers, review cadence for high-risk systems.
- Target skill: place a human decision point where the consequence is clinical.

### Exam 3 — Financial services and insurance (100 items)
- Case studies: Aurelian Capital, Fairgate Payments, Silverline Insurance, Kingsmere Bank.
- Focus: friction cost in regulated advice, fraud and claims operations, model risk documentation, agent governance, data residency, audit reconstruction.
- Microsoft apps: licence tiers for trading and service teams, Studio agents with connectors, Graph connectors for restricted records, Foundry Tools selection.
- Adoption: AI council decision rights, impact assessments, model cards, champions in regulated teams, commitment planning for steady workloads.
- Target skill: choose the measurement or control that actually gates a release.

### Exam 4 — Manufacturing, logistics, and energy (100 items)
- Case studies: Calder Works, Vantage Motors, Northline Logistics, Helios Energy.
- Focus: yield and demand forecasting, maintenance copilots, safety-critical review, retrieval over superseded work instructions, shift-peak capacity.
- Microsoft apps: Teams and SharePoint grounding, mobile chat, Studio agents with a CMMS connector, Foundry Tools for inspection and extraction.
- Adoption: shop-floor barriers, job-related fear, champions per site, retention of prompts and outputs, chargeback across plants.
- Target skill: distinguish "describe", "extract", and "decide" before choosing a capability.

### Exam 5 — Public sector and education (100 items)
- Case studies: Harborview Permits, Rowan University, Ashford Library, Metro Transit.
- Focus: citizen and student services, data residency, eligibility decisions with human oversight, accessibility, records retention, procurement.
- Microsoft apps: policy-grounded chat, in-app drafting, Studio permit agents, Graph connectors for case systems, streaming and speech.
- Adoption: departmental adoption team, champions per service, acceptable-use policy before shadow AI spreads, chargeback between services.
- Target skill: keep faithfulness, safety, and authorization as three separate arguments.

### Exam 6 — Cross-industry capstone (100 items)
- Case studies: Kestrel Group, Fairhaven Mutual, Brightpath Services, Highline Foods.
- Focus: shared standards across brands, permission-aware grounding, one compliance boundary, a group AI council, champions per brand, licensing across mixed usage.
- Recommended: attempt under a 45-minute timer with no notes, then review every explanation.
- Target skill: hold many constraints at once without collapsing them into one "best practice".

## Item formats and how to rehearse them

| Format | Per exam | How to practise |
|---|---:|---|
| Single-choice | 76 | Decide the constraint first, then eliminate options that solve a different problem |
| Multi-select | 16 | Count the required selections before reading options; reject "true but not asked" statements |
| Sequencing (exported as multiple-choice) | 8 | Find the step that cannot move — inventory, classify, pilot, measure — and anchor the rest |
| Case study | 4 × 3 | Read once, write the three or four hard constraints, then answer each item against them |

## Suggested eight-week schedule

| Week | Work | Deliverable |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Read domain 1 objectives, take Exam 1 in two halves | Mistake log by local skill |
| 2 | Read domain 2 objectives, take Exam 2 untimed | Rewrite five missed items as your own scenario |
| 3 | Re-read missed explanations from Exams 1–2, retake both timed | Two timed scores plus error categories |
| 4 | Domain 3 objectives, take Exam 3 | A one-page governance sketch for your own organization |
| 5 | Take Exam 4; review cost and capacity items carefully | Token, licence, and commitment cost table |
| 6 | Take Exam 5; review privacy and oversight items | Data classification and records-retention checklist |
| 7 | Take Exam 6 under a 45-minute clock | Timed score plus pace per item |
| 8 | Retake the weakest exam, review every explanation | Final readiness note, no score conversion |

## Scoring your own attempt

1. Score each item as correct or incorrect; there is no partial credit for multi-select.
2. Group misses by local skill ID, not by exam number.
3. Classify each miss: unknown product capability, misread constraint, plausible-but-wrong option, or rushed guess.
4. Only the last two categories improve with more practice tests; the first two need documentation study.
5. Re-attempt the same exam after a week before trusting an improvement.

A practice percentage is not a scaled exam score. Microsoft reports 700/1000 on a scaled scale and does not publish a raw percentage equivalent.

## Time budget inside a 100-item exam

| Segment | Items | Suggested time (pace drill: 45 min) |
|---|---:|---:|
| First pass, multiple-choice and multi-select | 76 | 30 minutes |
| Sequencing items (multiple-choice) | 8 | 5 minutes |
| Case studies | 12 | 7 minutes |
| Review of flagged items | 4 | 3 minutes |

The real appointment is 45 minutes and Microsoft does not publish an item count, so a 100-item attempt cannot reuse the real clock. The pace drill above is deliberately tighter than the real per-item allowance; the 90-minute option is for learning rather than timing.
