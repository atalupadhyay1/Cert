# Curriculum outline — Cloud Digital Leader practice course

**Six 100-item mock exams · 600 original questions · a 90-minute rehearsal that runs faster than the live paper**

This outline turns the bank into a study sequence. Every exam is blueprint-weighted **18 / 18 / 18 / 18 / 18 / 10** — the published weights themselves, which total exactly 100% — so the order below is about scenario complexity and topic reinforcement, not about weighting.

Google publishes a **50–60 question range**, a **90-minute** length, **no prerequisites**, and **no case-study component**. A 100-item exam is deliberately larger, which has one consequence worth stating plainly: **the bank runs faster than the live exam.** 100 items in 90 minutes is about **54 seconds per item**; the live 50–60 item paper allows roughly **90–108 seconds** each. Practise here under the 90-minute clock and you will be training at a stricter pace than the appointment itself.

## Learning arc

| Phase | Exam | What it stresses | Study goal |
|---|---|---|---|
| Vocabulary | 1 | Transformation terms, benefits, drivers, differentiators, cloud models, regions and zones, service models | Say what each term buys the business before reading options |
| Data | 2 | Data supply chain, silos, governance, storage classes, databases and warehouses, analytics | Decide which store or analytics product answers the stated need |
| AI | 3 | AI versus ML versus generative versus agentic AI, quality dimensions, responsible AI, model choice | Name the cheapest approach that satisfies the described task |
| Infrastructure | 4 | Migration strategies, compute and container choice, multicloud products, APIs | Separate "what must run" from "how managed it should be" |
| Trust | 5 | Threats, CIA, zero trust, encryption states, SecOps products, sovereignty | Match the control to the risk the stem names |
| Operations | 6 | Cost models, hierarchy, quotas, observability, reliability targets | Answer in business and financial language, not console language |

## Exam-by-exam study plan

### Exam 1 — Transformation drivers, cloud models, and business value (100 items)
- Official split: 18 / 18 / 18 / 18 / 18 / 10.
- Focus: agentic AI and generative AI as terms, open source and open standards, digital transformation as a change to how the business works, agility, scalability, cost, security, focus, data insights and global reach as benefits, cost and competitor and compliance and customer drivers, culture, skills, governance, and legacy as challenges, Google Cloud differentiators (openness, security by design, global network, data platform, AI leadership), private, hybrid, multicloud, and single-cloud models, regions, zones, edge locations, latency and bandwidth, and IaaS, PaaS, SaaS, and serverless.
- Target skill: read the business sentence, not the product name, and answer in terms of value, cost, or risk.

### Exam 2 — Data value, storage choice, and analytics (100 items)
- Focus: the value of data and the data supply chain from capture to use, silos and activation and interoperability, governance and lineage and quality gates, first-party and third-party data, structured, semi-structured, and unstructured data, databases versus warehouses versus lakes, Cloud Storage classes including Standard, Nearline, Coldline, Archive and Autoclass with retrieval fees, Cloud SQL, Spanner, AlloyDB, Bigtable, BigQuery, Firestore, Database Migration Service, rehost, replatform, refactor and retain for databases, Looker, Looker Studio, Pub/Sub, Dataflow, and Managed Service for Apache Spark.
- Target skill: for each stem, decide whether the need is transactional, analytical, archival, or event-driven — then choose the product.

### Exam 3 — AI concepts, model choice, and responsible use (100 items)
- Focus: AI, ML, generative AI, and agentic AI as distinct terms and distinct values, workforce, operations, customer support and scale as ML value, replacing rules and unstructured insight as ML use cases, accuracy, completeness, consistency, uniqueness, timeliness, and validity as quality dimensions, responsible AI with bias review, model cards, explainability and human review, choosing between a pre-trained API, Gemini, AutoML, BigQuery ML, and a custom model, Gemini Enterprise Agent Platform with Agent Studio, AutoML on Agent Platform, Model Garden and agent governance, AI Hypercomputer, accelerators, and flexible consumption.
- Target skill: say what the business wants before choosing how much model work it should own.

### Exam 4 — Migration, compute choice, and APIs (100 items)
- Focus: discovery before a path, rehost, replatform, refactor, reimagine, retain, and retire, virtual machines versus containers versus serverless versus microservices, Compute Engine and managed instance groups, autoscaling, load balancing, Spot VMs, GKE, Autopilot and GKE Enterprise, Cloud Run and Cloud Run functions, scale to zero, BigQuery Omni, AlloyDB Omni, APIs as a business product, gateways, Apigee, and monetisation.
- Target skill: match management burden to business appetite, and never answer with a configuration step.

### Exam 5 — Threats, controls, and earned trust (100 items)
- Focus: phishing, ransomware, denial of service, cryptomining, misconfiguration, and attacks on LLM applications, confidentiality, integrity, and availability, zero trust, least privilege and defense in depth, DLP, encryption at rest, in transit, and in use, authentication versus authorization versus auditing, IAM, Google Threat Intelligence, Security Command Center, Google Security Operations, Gemini in Security Operations, Mandiant and VirusTotal, AI Protection, Model Armor, the secure AI stack, VPC, Cloud Armor, Identity-Aware Proxy, Certificate Manager, Confidential Computing, secure access service edge, Access Transparency, transparency reports, third-party audits, sovereignty, residency, and the compliance resource manager.
- Target skill: name the risk first, then the control that reduces it, then the evidence it leaves.

### Exam 6 — Cost control, hierarchy, and reliability (100 items)
- Focus: CapEx to OpEx, total cost of ownership, idle capacity, budgets, labels, cost owners, billing accounts, the organisation, folder, and project hierarchy with inheritance, quotas, commitments, Spot VMs, Dynamic Workload Scheduler, Cloud Monitoring, Logging, Trace, and Error Reporting, redundancy and resilience, SLIs, SLOs, SLAs, error budgets, DevOps, and toil reduction.
- Recommended: attempt under the 90-minute timer with no notes, then review every explanation.
- Target skill: answer the way a finance or operations partner would, with the mechanism named but not configured.

## Item formats and how to rehearse them

| Format | Per exam | How to practise |
|---|---:|---|
| Single-answer | 84 | Identify the business constraint first, then eliminate options that solve a different problem |
| Multi-select | 16 | Count the required selections before reading options; reject "true but not asked" statements |
| Case study | 0 | Not part of this exam's published format, so not practised here |
| Drag-and-drop or sequencing | 0 | Not part of this exam's published format, so not practised here |

## Suggested six-week schedule

| Week | Work | Deliverable |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Study domain 1 objectives, take Exam 1 untimed | Mistake log by local skill ID |
| 2 | Domains 2 and 3, take Exams 2 and 3 untimed | One table: business need → data or AI product |
| 3 | Re-read missed explanations from Exams 1–3, retake Exam 2 timed | Timed score plus error categories |
| 4 | Domains 4 and 5, take Exams 4 and 5 | One sheet: risk → control → evidence |
| 5 | Domain 6, take Exam 6 under the 90-minute clock | Timed score plus pace per item |
| 6 | Retake the weakest exam, then read the 41-skill map cold | Readiness note, no score conversion |

## Scoring your own attempt

1. Score each item correct or incorrect; there is no partial credit for multi-select.
2. Group misses by local skill ID, not by exam number.
3. Classify each miss: unknown product, misread business constraint, plausible-but-wrong option, or rushed guess.
4. Only the last two categories improve with more practice tests; the first two need the official documentation or a conversation with a technical colleague.
5. Re-attempt the same exam after a week before trusting an improvement.

Google does not publish a passing score for the Cloud Digital Leader exam, so no percentage here converts to a pass mark and a practice percentage is not a scaled score.

## Time budget inside a 100-item exam

| Segment | Items | Suggested time (90-minute clock) |
|---|---:|---:|
| First pass, single-answer | 84 | 62 minutes |
| Multi-select | 16 | 18 minutes |
| Review of flagged items | — | 10 minutes |

The live exam delivers 50–60 questions in 90 minutes, which is 90–108 seconds per item — noticeably more relaxed than this bank's 54 seconds. Finishing this set inside the clock is therefore **harder** than finishing the real appointment, so do not read a rushed practice attempt as evidence that you are slow. Conversely, a comfortable timed run here is a good sign.

## What this course does not cover, on purpose

- **No configuration objectives.** Google's CDL guide contains none, so no item asks for a subnet, a policy, a role binding, or a tuning parameter. The exam assesses business value and use-case matching, and so does this bank. Unlike the engineering courses in this repository, there is no "configuration is in scope" exception to document.
- **No case studies.** The published format has no case-study component.
- **No lab or console tasks.** They cannot exist in a CSV, and they are not part of this exam's published format in any case.
- **No passing score.** Google does not publish one, so none is stated.
