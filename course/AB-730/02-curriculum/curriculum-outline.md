# Curriculum outline — AB-730 practice course

**Six mock exams · 100 items each · 600 in total · four domains at 28 / 24 / 24 / 24 · 38 local skills**

This course is built for the business user preparing for **Microsoft Certified: AI Business Professional (AB-730)**. The exam is a 45-minute proctored assessment that passes at 700/1000 and publishes no item count. The sequence below turns six 100-item exams into a study programme rather than a pile of questions.

## Learning arc

| Stage | Exams | What you are practising | What good looks like by the end |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1. Foundations | 1 | Data protection, context, choosing an experience, verification | You can say what an answer was allowed to see and why it should be checked |
| 2. Prompt craft | 2 | Prompt anatomy, sources, saving, scheduling, sharing, memory, chats, Notebooks | You can turn a vague request into a prompt a colleague could reuse |
| 3. Content and meetings | 3 | Connectors, cross-app content, visuals, meeting preparation, recap, Pages | You can move work from source to reviewed artifact without losing the thread |
| 4. Agents and delegation | 4 | Prebuilt and custom agents, Cowork, skills, checkpoints, scheduled tasks | You can choose the lightest tool that fits and keep control of a delegated run |
| 5. Sector rehearsal | 5 | Media, marketing, nonprofit, events | You can apply the same judgement to unfamiliar business context |
| 6. Capstone | 6 | Cross-divisional, regulated, group-level decisions | You can defend a choice under conflicting constraints |

## Exam-by-exam study plan

### Exam 1 — Cross-industry baseline (100 items)

*Cases: Brightline Retail, Harborview Claims, Cedar Health, Meridian Bank.*

Study focus: the four moves that decide most AB-730 items — what the answer is grounded on, who can see it, how it will be verified, and which Copilot experience performs the task.

Before you start: write down your own organization's rule for putting client or employee data into a prompt. You will meet that decision repeatedly.

After the exam: list every item you missed that turned on a permission or a source rather than on product knowledge. Those are the highest-value fixes.

### Exam 2 — Professional services (100 items)

*Cases: Larkspur Consulting, Wexford Legal, Bluepeak Accounting, Halden Design.*

Study focus: prompt craft as a professional habit — audience, constraints, examples, output format, source references, model choice — then the lifecycle around a prompt: save it, schedule it, share it, refine it, and know what memory carries forward.

Drill: take one prompt you wrote this week and rewrite it with a named audience, two source references, and an output format. Compare the two outputs.

### Exam 3 — Manufacturing, logistics, and energy (100 items)

*Cases: Calder Manufacturing, Northline Freight, Vantage Foods, Ironwood Energy.*

Study focus: content produced from real data. Reconciling generated figures with the workbook, building a chart from the range rather than a screenshot, working across Word, Excel, and PowerPoint, and keeping a meeting's decisions attached to owners.

Drill: produce a one-page summary from a spreadsheet, then trace every number back to a cell. Note how long the trace took; that is the real cost of unverified output.

### Exam 4 — Public sector and education (100 items)

*Cases: Rowan Council, Ashford University, Metro Transit, Lakeshore Library.*

Study focus: regulated and public-facing work. Retention and record class, access review before enablement, DLP and sensitivity labels, citations a reviewer can open, and meeting records that survive scrutiny.

Drill: for one document you own, decide its record class, retention period, and who should be able to open it. Then check whether Copilot would respect all three.

### Exam 5 — Media, marketing, and nonprofit (100 items)

*Cases: Trellis Media, Brightpath Agency, Solstice Foundation, Kestrel Events.*

Study focus: creative and campaign work. Image generation and usage rights, brand checks, external archives and connectors, turning a recorded call into a brief, and shipping one live draft through Pages.

Drill: take a generated image or paragraph and write the three checks you would need before publishing it. If you cannot name the rights holder, the check is incomplete.

### Exam 6 — Cross-industry capstone (100 items)

*Cases: Highline Group, Fairhaven Financial, Evergreen Pharma, Atlas Distribution.*

Study focus: integrated decisions under conflicting constraints. One framework for choosing between Chat, an agent, and Cowork; an incident response for oversharing; a containment response for prompt injection; agent ownership; and scheduled work that someone actually owns.

Drill: write a one-page decision framework you could hand to a colleague: task type, sources, tool, control, approver. Then test it against all four capstone cases.

## Item formats and how to rehearse them

| Format | Count per exam | How to answer | How to rehearse |
|---|---:|---|---|
| Multiple choice | 76 (including 8 sequencing) | One key, always in slots 1–4 of the original file; the player may shuffle | Read all four options before answering; the near-miss is usually a control that is real but wrong for this constraint |
| Multi-select | 16 | Two keys, comma-separated, with the count stated in the stem | Check each candidate against the constraint separately; do not stop at the first two that sound sensible |
| Sequencing | 8, exported as multiple choice | One candidate order is correct | Find the step that must come first and the artifact each later step needs; eliminate orders that review before producing |
| Case study | 4 blocks × 3 | Three independently scored items sharing one scenario | Read the scenario once, then answer each item against its own stated constraint |

## Suggested eight-week schedule

| Week | Work | Time | Output |
|---:|---|---:|---|
| 1 | Read the official study guide; take Exam 1 untimed | 3 h | A list of the five constraints you missed |
| 2 | Re-read Exam 1 explanations; drill domain 1 | 3 h | A one-page data-protection rule for your own work |
| 3 | Take Exam 2; drill prompt craft | 3 h | Two reusable prompts saved and shared |
| 4 | Take Exam 3; drill content and figures | 3 h | One traced report and one chart built from data |
| 5 | Take Exam 4; drill records and review | 3 h | A record-class and access note for one document set |
| 6 | Take Exam 5; drill creative and external sources | 3 h | A rights-and-brand checklist for generated assets |
| 7 | Take Exam 6; drill agents and Cowork | 3 h | A one-page tool-choice framework with approvers |
| 8 | Retake your weakest exam cold; review every miss | 3 h | A final one-page readiness note |

The schedule assumes roughly three hours a week. If you have less time, keep the exams and drop the drills; if you have more, retake each exam after a week rather than reading the same explanations twice.

## Scoring your own attempt

1. **Raw score** — correct items out of 100. Useful for tracking, not for predicting the assessment.
2. **Domain score** — correct items per official domain. A weak domain with 24 items is 24% of the exam, so two or three misses there matter more than they feel.
3. **Constraint score** — for each miss, write the constraint in one line. If you cannot, the miss is a knowledge gap, not a judgement error, and the fix is different.
4. **Format score** — count misses by item type. A pattern in multi-select usually means you accepted a plausible-sounding control without testing it against the constraint.
5. **Second attempt** — retake after at least a week. A score that holds up cold is the only one worth acting on.

**Do not convert a practice percentage into a 700/1000 equivalent.** Microsoft scores the live assessment on a scaled scale and publishes no raw percentage mapping. The passing target is stated here only as the published fact.

## Time budget inside a 100-item exam

The live assessment gives 45 minutes and publishes no item count. For practice, use one of two modes:

- **Pace drill — 45 minutes for 100 items.** That is 27 seconds per item. Most items are solvable in that time if you read the constraint first; case studies need more, so bank time on the short items.
- **Review attempt — 90 minutes.** Read every explanation after each block of 25, and write the constraint for every miss while the reasoning is still fresh.

A practical split for the pace drill: 35 minutes for the standalone items, 10 minutes for the four case blocks. If you are still reading options at 40 minutes, stop and score what you have — the lesson is in the pacing, not in finishing.
